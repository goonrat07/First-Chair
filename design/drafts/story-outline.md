# Draft: First Chair's story, a more specific outline

> **Status:** Draft for the user to choose from (sleep mode, 2026-11-02, at the user's request: "brainstorming a more
> specific outline of the story"). Nothing here is decided. The game brief keeps Career and story out of the MVP;
> this is for after riding feels right. Companion drafts: `gear-progression.md`, `skill-tree.md`.

## What it starts from

- **The brief's pitch:** "A warm, sunlit skiing game where you carve, jump and thread the trees of a small family-run
  mountain, and every run you ride can be replayed exactly." Players who want it warm and forgiving, where a crash
  costs a second.
- **Bluebird's story** (`design/reference/bluebird-spec.md`, "Story and missions"): *The Last Season at Kettle Peak*.
  A family mountain one bad winter from being sold to Summit Corp; you arrive as the new liftie; four acts (Opening
  Day, Summit Comes Calling, Storm Cycle, The Kettle Classic); four crews and a filmer; Cass, Summit's sponsored rider,
  as a rival to the end; the Mountain Pulse (how alive the mountain is) sets how big the win is; June never sells.
  Its tone rules stay good: warm first, funny in the details, real where it matters (avalanches, the patrol), short
  lines, a silent rider called by a nickname.
- **What First Chair has now:** skis only (snowboard later); a test mountain with a chairlift, runs, a park and
  trees, and a second mountain (Pine Bowl) from the mountain tool; carving, tucks, jumps, grabs, spins, flips, rails,
  trees and Flow; a Steep-style chase camera; snow that holds real tracks.

## What other snow games show

| Game | Story and structure | What to take or avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Grand Mountain Adventure 2 | No story. Collect ski passes and do challenges to open the next mountain; credits buy gear and clothes; some clothes behind the hardest medals ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6475040075), [press release](https://gamespress.com/Grand-Mountain-Adventure-2-the-sequel-to-the-Worlds-Most-Downloaded-Sk)) | Opening places is the reward that keeps you riding. The story should open places, not replace that |
| Shredders (2022) | A linear, tongue-in-cheek campaign about two YouTubers; a friend introduces pros who teach moves; missions open areas. Reviews split on the cast and voice acting ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shredders_(video_game)), [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/game-reviews/shredders-review-3185454)) | Characters teaching moves works; a loud cast wears thin. Keep lines short and optional |
| Steep: Road to the Olympics | A documentary-style campaign; reviewers called its rigid structure alien to Steep's free roaming ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/steep_road_to_the_olympics/b/playstation4/archive/2017/12/13/game-informer-review-steep-road-to-the-olympics)) | A strict sequence fights a free-riding game. Missions should start where you already ride |
| Riders Republic | Careers per sport, with sponsors and big events along each ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/riders-republic-sponsors-work-unlock-earn/)) | Parallel tracks let a park rider and a powder rider progress at the same pace (Bluebird's tier-ups do the same) |
| Lonely Mountains: Snow Riders | No story; new mountains open by riding them nearly perfectly ([Way Too Many Games](https://waytoomany.games/2025/01/21/review-lonely-mountains-snow-riders/)) | Mastery as the gate suits a skill game, but needs a gentler path for the warm audience |

**So:** the story's job is to give the season a shape and open the mountain piece by piece. It should never stand
between the player and the snow: no cutscenes, scenes only when the rider stops, every mission started where the
player already rides.

## Three directions

### A. The Last Season (Bluebird's story, trimmed for skis first)

The mountain is about to be sold; you help win it back by spring. Keep the four acts, June, the crews, Cass and the
Kettle Classic. Trim for First Chair's scale: one sport until the snowboard lands (Cass skis), Mountain Ops and Heat
later or never, the road trip as post-game.

- **For:** fully written already (missions, lines, rewards), with clear stakes and a finale.
- **Against:** the corporate villain and the chases are a lot of systems (Heat, pursuers, After Hours) before the
  core is proven; "save the mountain from developers" is a familiar plot.

### B. First Chair (new, cosier, no villain)

You're the new liftie at a family hill. Every morning you run first chair, and the season's story is the mountain
waking up: each act opens a lift, and its first chair is the act's opening scene. The tension comes from the
mountain itself (a thin early season, a storm cycle, an avalanche played straight) and a friendly rival, not from a
company. The finale is the season's last day: the Last Chair party and race.

- **For:** the title becomes the structure; fewer systems (no pursuers); fits "warm and forgiving" exactly.
- **Against:** softer stakes. The drama has to come from places, people and weather, which takes good writing.

### C. Ski-bum road trip

A season spent on several small mountains (each made with the mountain tool), each a short arc with its own local
crew and one event; the thread is a filmer cutting your season's video part.

- **For:** each mountain is a self-contained chunk, easy to build one at a time; it uses the mountain tool well.
- **Against:** no home mountain to care about; less of the "family hill" warmth the pitch is built on.

**Recommended: B as the spine, A's people and tone, C as the post-game.** Keep June, Mo, the crew leaders, Hank and
Bowie, Tavi and a rival; drop the corporate antagonist (or keep Summit as a one-act comic subplot, decision 2 below);
after the finale, road trips to other mountains. The rest of this file outlines that.

## Outline of the recommended direction

**Premise.** Juniper Ridge (working name; Kettle Peak is free to keep) is a small family-run hill reopening after a
bad winter. June Alder hires you to run the chairlift. If the hill has a good season, it stays open; how good is the
**Mountain Pulse** (Bluebird's number, kept: crowds, posters, the radio and the ending's size read it; it only goes
up). Every act opens a lift, and every lift's first ride is a short scene with June, Mo and whoever the act is about.

**The cast** (Bluebird's, trimmed to eight): June (owner, dry), Mo (your shift partner, gossip), Dex (builds the park,
puns), Wren (glades, few words), Ilse (ex-racer, timing hut), Hank (patrol, deadpan, with Bowie the avalanche dog),
Tavi (filmer, talks in shot lists), and **Cass**, the rival: a hotshot skier from the next valley's big resort. Cass's
arc is respect, not conversion (Bluebird D4).

| Act | Season | Lift that opens (first chair scene) | Places | What the act teaches | Main beats (4 to 6 missions) | Set piece |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue: First Chair | Opening morning, before dawn | The Express: your first first chair, alone with June at sunrise | Bunny hill, one groomer | Carve, tuck, stop, the chase camera | Load the first chair; ride the opening groomer; meet Mo | The sunrise ride up |
| 1. Opening Day | Early season, thin snow | The Express, for the public | Groomers, the small park, the first glade | Jumps and grabs, rails, trees and Flow | Meet each crew at its hangout; one starter challenge each; Tavi's first clip | The first crowd at the lift line |
| 2. The Upper Mountain | Midwinter | The Summit chair (shut two seasons; you and Hank reopen it) | Steeps, the race course, the full park, black glades | Racing lines, bigger air, combos | Ilse's first race; Dex's Big Air; Cass shows up and wins; Wren's black glade | The night the Summit chair runs again |
| 3. Storm Cycle | Deep winter | The Ridge T-bar (hike-to terrain) | Powder bowls, the couloir, backcountry gates | Powder float, deep snow, beacon and probe | Hank's safety training; three-day storm cycle; the burial (played straight); Cass and you ride the bowl together | The avalanche rescue |
| 4. Last Chair | Spring | (none: every lift runs) | Slush afternoons, the pond skim | Everything, under pressure | Each crew's practice for the festival; Tavi cuts your video part; the festival races against Cass | The Last Chair: the season's final ride up, at sunset, with everyone |
| After: Road trips | Free days | The bus stop | Other mountains (Pine Bowl first) | Mountain by mountain | A local crew and one event per mountain | Each mountain's own last run |

**How it is told** (Bluebird's delivery, kept): phone texts for most story, scenes of 30 seconds at most and only
when the rider stops, mission markers at the cast's hangouts, an optional radio. No line over about 20 words.

**How it gates** (Bluebird D1, kept): an act opens when its main missions are done and the player has enough
progress on any mix of tracks (crew tiers in Bluebird), so a park rider and a powder rider reach act 2 together.

**Options for acts 3 and 4:**

- **The burial** (3-6): played straight as above, lighter (the "guest" is a crash-test dummy in Hank's drill), or
  left out (the act turns on the storm alone). Recommended: as above, short, nobody hurt; it gives the act its
  weight and pays off 3-1.
- **The festival's results** (4-8): fixed (you win one, Cass wins one, a local wins one), or played for real with
  any result accepted. Recommended: for real; the ending doesn't change, only the trophies.
- **Out of the MVP and needed here:** weather and time of day changing the snow, surface lifts, combos, the replay
  editor, water. Each is its own story; acts 3 and 4 can't ship before them.

## What the story needs that doesn't exist yet

- A **game layer** that reads sim events after each step (missions, payouts, the story director). Bluebird's rule
  stays: menus, rewards and story never touch the sim.
- **Lifts that open by act**, and a mountain with the act's places (the mountain tool can lay them out).
- **Other riders**: Cass's races need sim bots (Bluebird's `bots.ts`).
- **People**: the cast as models (the character pipeline that built CH_Skier), standing at hangouts.
- **A save**, and the Mountain Pulse computed from it.

## Decisions for the user

1. Which direction: A, B, C, or the recommended B with A's people and C as the post-game?
2. A villain or not: keep Summit Corp (comic, act 2 only, or the whole arc), or no company at all?
3. Keep Kettle Peak and Bluebird's cast names, or new ones?
4. The player: a silent custom rider called by a nickname (Bluebird D6), or a named character?
5. Length: Bluebird aimed at 12 to 15 hours; a first version could be the prologue and act 1 (about 2 hours).
6. Is "warm and funny" (Bluebird D3) still the tone?
