# Look notes from Grand Mountain Adventure 2 (user's screenshots, 2026-10-20)

The user shared five GMA2 screenshots as inspiration: two skier close-ups, a high follow-camera shot on a slope, a village
with a railway, and a foggy glade. The screenshots are copyrighted, so they are not stored in this repo
(`.claude/skills/blender-image-to-3d/FIRST-CHAIR.md`: commit only references we made or that are CC0). These notes are
our own description of what they show. Use them as the brief for our own reference sheets and models: take the feel,
proportions and palette, never copy a design one to one.

## Overall look

- **Shapes.** Low-poly, faceted, mostly flat-shaded geometry with almost no texture detail. Read comes from silhouette,
  flat colour and lighting.
- **Light.** A strong, low sun gives long, crisp shadows. Shadows on snow are clearly blue to blue-violet, never grey.
  Lit snow is near-white with a faint warm cast.
- **Atmosphere.** White fog builds with distance, so far trees fade to pale silhouettes. Edges of the frame blur in the
  high-camera shots, like a tilt-shift depth of field.
- **Snow.** Fine, thin, parallel grooves of ski tracks everywhere, plus boot prints in soft snow. Powder spray is
  chunky white low-poly clumps, not particles.
- **Colour.** Snow, sky and shadows are cool and calm; characters, gear and a few trees carry the saturated colour.

## Skier (close-up, front three-quarter, on a dark grey backdrop)

- **Proportions.** Chunky and slightly stylised: a large rounded helmet, a boxy oversized jacket, baggy trousers. Tall
  enough to read as an adult, but the head and hands are a little big for the body.
- **Stance.** Athletic: knees bent and pushed forward, hips back a little, arms held slightly out from the sides with
  the hands low and forward, poles trailing back. Skis parallel, about a hip-width apart.
- **Helmet.** Grey to blue-grey shell with a lighter top panel. Large goggles with a dark frame and a bright yellow
  lens. A dark navy neck warmer covers the lower face.
- **Jacket.** Red-orange, around `#D4512C`. A hood collar and a white centre zip line. Two short vertical white
  stripes on the chest, one each side of the zip. One white stripe runs down the outside of each upper sleeve. Dark
  cuffs.
- **Gloves.** Chunky, mitten-like, saturated yellow (`#E3CF3A`).
- **Trousers.** Muted yellow-khaki to olive (`#C3B45C`), baggy. Darker olive rounded knee patches catch the light as
  separate facets.
- **Boots.** Dark slate shells, with a rust-orange band at the cuff where the trousers meet the boot.
- **Poles.** Thin dark shafts, small baskets, held angled back.
- **Skis.** Teal-green (`#5E9E86`) with a lighter green top-sheet graphic: small X marks near the tip and tail, and
  rows of short horizontal stripes. Fairly wide, with twin-tip-like rounded ends.

## Second skier (close-up in a deep crouch, user's screenshot 2)

- **Face.** The lower face shows under the goggles: a simple faceted nose, mouth and chin in a light-medium skin
  tone. The head reads as a person, not a helmet with a scarf. Our skier should show the lower face too, with the
  neck warmer pulled down or left off.
- **Head gear.** A teal knit beanie with a darker folded cuff band and a tan pom-pom on top, with the goggle strap
  around it. The goggles are big, with an amber lens and a light frame. A beanie is a good second head option next to
  the helmet.
- **Jacket.** Teal-blue and very puffy, rounded at the shoulders, with a hood bunched up at the back of the neck and a
  light centre zip. It carries a darker zigzag (chevron) pattern. A simple pattern like this suits the flat-colour
  style, as a texture or vertex-colour bands.
- **Trousers and gloves.** Orange-tan, baggy, big rounded knees. Gloves are the same tan.
- **Poles.** Orange shafts, blue grips, and big chunky octagonal baskets that read clearly at a distance.
- **Skis and boots.** Long orange-brown skis with a darker top-sheet pattern; light grey-blue boots.
- **Stance.** A deep crouch: hips back and low, back rounded forward, hands wide and forward with the poles angled
  back. The long jacket covers the hips.
- **Proportions.** The head is modest next to the bulky jacket; most of the volume is in the torso and shoulders.
  The user is happy with our current proportions; take the face, hood and puffiness from this, not a resize.

## Trees (slope shot and glade shot)

- **Conifers.** Tall, very slender trunks with sparse, drooping branch tiers all the way up. Each tier is a few
  narrow, angled planes rather than a solid cone, so light and sky show between tiers. The crown is a narrow spike.
- **Snow load.** On the slope, most trees are heavily snow-laden: tiers read white to pale blue on top, dark green
  underneath. A few dark green spruces have little snow.
- **Glade variety.** Olive-green needle trees, rust and orange-brown trees (larch-like or dead), and a few clumpy
  rounded brown crowns on thin trunks. Fallen dead logs are dark grey-brown poles lying across the snow.
- **Shadows.** Long, thin, blue tree shadows streak across the snow and help read the slope direction.
- **For First Chair:** a conifer at 300 to 1.5k tris (FIRST-CHAIR.md budget) with tiered plane-like branches, snow
  caps as a separate light material, and colour variants: snowy spruce, bare olive, rust larch.

## Village and props (village shot)

- **Houses.** Dark blue-grey wooden cladding with white trim around the windows and corners. Steep roofs with thick,
  rounded snow caps hanging slightly over the edges, and small chimneys. Warm lit windows at dusk.
- **Railway.** Dark rails on dark sleepers cutting across the snow, a dark train car, simple street lamps, wooden
  fences and railings.
- **Lift.** Grey steel towers, thin cables, small grey gondola cabins with a dark window band.
- **Rock.** Dark grey rock bands show at the edges of steep rollers and cliffs.

## Gameplay camera (user's three gameplay screenshots, re-sent 2026-10-23)

Measured from a snowboard run at sunset, a skier on a forested slope with a gondola, and a skier crossing a railway in
the village:
- **Direction.** Straight behind the rider, uphill, looking down the line of travel: the rider's own tracks run from
  the bottom centre of the screen up to the rider. Not offset to one side.
- **Framing.** The rider is centred left to right and sits a little below the middle (about 55 to 70 percent down the
  screen). The slope ahead, downhill, fills the upper half up to a distant valley or village near the top edge. Little
  or no sky.
- **Height and size.** Looks down at about 40 degrees from far away: the rider is only about 4 percent of the screen
  height (25 px of 600). The narrow field of view flattens the scene a little, like a telephoto lens.
- **Readability cues.** Ski tracks everywhere, old and new, and the rider's own fresh pair; trees and rocks with long,
  crisp blue shadows from a low sun; haze toward the distance; a tilt-shift blur at the top and bottom edges; snow
  shading that shows every roller and lip.
- **HUD.** Small white condensed labels in a top corner (GATE, TARGET, TIME), or a points counter top left.

## HUD (for later)

White, bold, condensed uppercase labels with a soft drop shadow, small and in the top-right corner (for example
GATE / TARGET / TIME with values below each).
